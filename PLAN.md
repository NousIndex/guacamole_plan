# Training Plan

**Equipment:** 2 adjustable dumbbells, **10 kg max each** · doorway pull-up bar · jump rope
**Lifter:** 66 kg, 165 cm
**Baseline (week 0):** 0 pull-ups · 6s dead hang · 10 push-ups · 5 × 1 min rope w/ 1 min rest · 10 min farmer hold @ 5.5 kg/hand

Goal: hit every major muscle group twice a week, and put the pull-up on a
dedicated progression instead of leaving it to chance.

---

## The constraint that shapes everything

Your dumbbells top out at **20 kg total — about 30% of your bodyweight.** A
push-up already loads you with roughly 42 kg. So for legs and chest, the
dumbbells are not, and never will be, the hard part.

That means **load is not your progression variable.** In order of what to
reach for as things get easy:

1. **Unilateral** — one leg or one arm at a time doubles the relative load
   (a 10 kg single-leg RDL is worth a 20 kg two-leg one, and then some).
2. **Tempo** — 3 seconds down. Same weight, far more tension.
3. **Pauses** — 2 seconds at the hardest position, dead stop, no bounce.
4. **Reps** — 12 → 15 → 20 before you write a set off as pointless.
5. **Shorter rest** — 90s → 60s → 45s on the same weight is a real overload.

Only the pressing and pulling work has room to grow by weight, and even there
you'll cap out within a few months. That's fine — bodyweight work on the bar
and the floor is what carries you from there.

## Weekly schedule

| Day | Session | Time |
|-----|---------|------|
| Mon | **Upper A — Pull** | ~40 min |
| Tue | **Lower + Core** | ~30 min |
| Wed | **Conditioning** (rope) | ~27 min |
| Thu | **Upper B — Push** | ~40 min |
| Fri | **Lower + Core** (2nd pass — add reps or slow the tempo) | ~30 min |
| Sat | **Easy Cardio** or full rest | ~13 min |
| Sun | Rest | — |

**Daily Practice** (12 min) is optional but it's the single biggest lever on
the pull-up. Do it Tue / Wed / Sat — any day that isn't Upper A or Upper B.
It's deliberately easy: never go near failure.

Short on time? Mon / Wed / Fri as Pull → Lower → Push is a complete week.

### Coverage check
- **Back / lats / biceps:** hangs, negatives, inverted rows, one-arm rows, curls (Mon + daily)
- **Chest / shoulders / triceps:** push-ups, floor press, OHP, laterals, dips, pullover (Thu)
- **Quads / glutes / hams / calves:** split squat, goblet squat, single-leg RDL, hip thrust, calf raise (Tue + Fri)
- **Core:** dead bug, side plank, suitcase carry (Tue + Wed)
- **Grip / forearms:** hangs, towel hangs, suitcase carry (Mon + Wed + daily)
- **Conditioning:** rope (Wed + Sat)

## Starting weights

Every number below is **kg per dumbbell**, and each is already set as the
default in the app. They're deliberately conservative — set 1 should feel like
you had 3 more reps in you. Adjust after your first session and don't look back.

| Exercise | Start | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| One-arm DB row | **10 kg** (max) | Already your ceiling. Progress with a 3s lower, then a 2s pause at the top, then 15 reps. |
| Goblet squat | **10 kg** | 3s down, 2s pause. Light on purpose — the tempo is the exercise. |
| Single-leg DB RDL | **10 kg** | Held in the opposite hand to the working leg. Balance limits you before the weight does. |
| Bulgarian split squat | **7.5 kg** each | The most useful leg exercise you own. Go to 10 kg each when 3 × 10 is clean. |
| Single-leg calf raise | **10 kg** | One dumbbell, hold something for balance. 2s pause at the top. |
| DB floor press | **10 kg** each | Too light to be your main press — that's the push-ups. This is 12 slow reps for volume. |
| Seated overhead press | **7.5 kg** each | The one dumbbell lift where 10 kg will be a genuine challenge. Get to 4 × 10, then move up. |
| Overhead tricep extension | **7.5 kg** | One dumbbell, both hands — or 5 kg in each hand. |
| DB pullover | **10 kg** | One dumbbell, both hands. |
| Lateral raise / reverse fly | **4 kg** | Yes, that light. If it's swinging, it's too heavy. |
| DB thruster | **7.5 kg** each | Conditioning, not strength — keep moving. |
| Suitcase carry | **10 kg** | One hand, stay perfectly upright, swap each set. |

**When to add weight:** you hit the top of the rep range on *every* set with
1–2 reps still in the tank. Then add the smallest plate you have and drop back
to the bottom of the range. Once you're at 10 kg you're capped — switch to the
progression ladder at the top of this document instead.

## The pull-up progression

Your farmer's hold changes the read here. **10 minutes at 5.5 kg/hand means
grip endurance is not your limiter** — that's 11 kg total, where a dead hang
puts 30–40 kg through each hand. What you're missing is grip at *near-
bodyweight load*, plus the shoulder and lat endurance to stay under the bar.
That's also why there are no farmer's holds in this plan: at your ceiling they
can't get heavy enough to matter. Hanging is the grip work now.

The good news: **66 kg at 165 cm is a favourable build for pull-ups.** There
isn't much of you to lift. This is a months-long project, not a years-long one.

**Stage 1 — Own the hang (weeks 1–4).** Target: 30s.
- *Max hangs:* 3 sets, full 90s rest. Start at 4–5s (≈70% of your best), not 6.
  Add ~2s per set per week.
- *Feet-assisted hangs:* toes on a chair taking just enough weight that you can
  hold 45s. This is where the endurance actually gets built — you can't
  accumulate volume out of 6-second sets. Take less weight off the chair each week.
- Use chalk, or hang a towel over the bar, if your skin gives out before your grip.

**Stage 2 — Scap pulls + negatives (week 2 onward).**
- *Scapular pull:* hang with straight arms, pull your shoulder blades down and
  back so your body rises 2–3 cm. No elbow bend. 3 × 5.
- *Negative:* step off a chair into the top position (chin over bar), then lower
  under control. Start with a 3s lower, build to 5s, then 8s. 3 × 3, and end the
  set the moment a lower turns into a drop.

**Stage 3 — First rep (roughly week 6–10).** When you can hold a 20s hang and
control an 8s negative, test one pull-up at the very start of Upper A, fully
fresh. Once you get it, replace negatives with `Pull-Up — 5 sets × 1 rep, 2 min
rest`, and add one rep to one set each week.

**Inverted rows are your best substitute.** If your doorway bar can be mounted
low in the frame — many can — or you can lie under a sturdy table and pull your
chest to the edge, that gives you a horizontal pull at a scalable fraction of
your bodyweight, which is exactly what 10 kg dumbbells cannot. Make it harder by
walking your feet further out or elevating them. If neither setup works in your
place, do the one-arm rows for 15 slow reps instead and lean harder on negatives.

## Push-ups: 10 → 20

Training to failure at 10 is why you're stuck at 10. Do volume at ~60% of max:
**5 × 6 with 60s rest** on Upper B, plus 4 × 5 in Daily Practice. Weekly reps go
from ~30 to ~70 without a single grinding set.

Your dumbbells can't overload a press — 20 kg is less than half what a push-up
already asks of you — so push-ups *are* the chest program, and the floor press
is accessory volume.

Re-test your max every 3 weeks, first thing on Upper B, then reset the working
sets to ~60% of the new number. Past 15, switch the working sets to
feet-elevated push-ups and restart the count. If a set of 6 ever feels hard,
you're too deep into fatigue — cut it to 5.

## Jump rope: 5 × 1 min → 10 min unbroken

You currently rest as long as you work (1:1). Shrink the rest before you
lengthen the work:

| Weeks | Intervals |
|-------|-----------|
| 1–2 | 6 × 60s, 45s rest |
| 3–4 | 6 × 60s, 30s rest |
| 5–6 | 5 × 90s, 30s rest |
| 7–8 | 4 × 2 min, 30s rest |
| 9+ | 3 × 3 min, 45s rest → then chase 10 min unbroken |

Trips don't end the interval — pick the rope up and keep going until the timer
stops. Count total time skipping, not consecutive jumps.

---

## Deloads

Every 6–8 weeks, or any time two sessions in a row go backwards: same workouts,
2/3 of the weight, stop 3 reps short of your normal stopping point. One week,
then resume where you left off.

---

## Track these four numbers

Log them in the app and re-check every 3 weeks:

1. Longest single dead hang
2. Max push-ups in one set
3. Pull-ups (0 → 1 → …)
4. Longest unbroken jump rope

Everything else is in service of these.

## Movements you may not know

- **Feet-assisted hang** — hang from the bar with your toes on a chair,
  supporting just enough weight to hold 45 seconds. Volume you can't get out of
  6-second max hangs.
- **Towel hang** — drape a towel over the bar and grip the two ends. Much harder
  on the grip at the same bodyweight, which is how you overload grip without
  heavier dumbbells.
- **Inverted row** — bar mounted low, or lie under a table; body straight, pull
  your chest to the bar. A horizontal pull-up.
- **DB floor press** — bench press lying on the floor. Your elbows stop at the
  ground, which is also what protects your shoulders without a bench.
- **DB pullover** — lie on the floor, both hands on one dumbbell, arms straight,
  lower it back past your head. Lats and serratus.
- **Bulgarian split squat** — rear foot up on a chair, dumbbells at your sides,
  drop straight down. Brutal at light weight, which is exactly why it's here.
- **Single-leg hip thrust** — shoulders on the floor, one foot planted, other
  knee tucked, drive your hips up. Glutes without a bench.
- **Chair dip** — hands on a chair edge behind you, feet out front. Keep the
  chair against a wall.
- **Suitcase carry march** — one dumbbell in one hand, march in place staying
  perfectly upright. Anti-lateral-flexion core plus grip.
- **Thruster** — front squat straight into an overhead press.

Warm up before every session: 2–3 min easy rope, arm circles, 10 bodyweight
squats, 5 slow push-ups.
